Does NTD require a GTFS feed?

What
Yes — if you operate fixed route service. Those reporters must maintain a public GTFS feed and submit the link on Form P-50 with the Annual Report. shapes.txt is now required too: RY 2025 for Full Reporters, RY 2026 for everyone else.
Who
Full, Reduced (including Tribal), and Rural subrecipient reporters — but only where they operate a fixed route mode. Not Reduced Asset or Group Plan Sponsor reporters, and not a demand-response requirement.
When
With your Annual Report, due four months after your fiscal year ends. For a 30 June fiscal year end — about three in five NTD reporters — that is 31 October.

Everything on this page is sourced to FTA and the Federal Register; the citations are listed at the bottom. Your NTD validation analyst is the authority on your specific report.

The deadline

There is no single national NTD deadline. The 2026 NTD Policy Manual puts it plainly: “FTA determines each agency’s NTD report due date based on the agency’s fiscal year-end date. Reporters submit their Annual Report four months after their fiscal year ends.”

In practice that produces three dates, and one of them dominates. In FTA’s published 2024 Agency Information file, 1,742 of the 2,908 reporter records carrying a due date — 59.9% — were due 31 October, because 59% of reporters run a fiscal year ending 30 June.

Two things about that table are easy to miss.

  • The waiver deadline comes first. The manual sets the waiver and special-request deadline two months ahead of the due date — 31 August for a 30 June fiscal year end — and separately states that waivers must be requested 60 days before the Annual Report is due. If you already know you cannot produce a conforming feed in time, that is the date that matters, not the due date. FTA approves waivers case by case and will not approve one on the grounds of cost, staffing, or data-collection difficulty.
  • Revisions are not an extension. The last two columns are a window for you and your validation analyst to resolve questions about a report you already filed. A report not submitted by the due date is late, and a late report is one of the four things that can draw a Failure to Report notice.

What FTA actually requires

The requirement lives on Form P-50, “General Transit Feed Specification Data for Fixed Route Service.” FTA collects it under 49 U.S.C. § 5335, which the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act amended to add “geographic service area coverage” to the categories the National Transit Database accumulates. FTA adopted GTFS as the mechanism, and made it mandatory beginning in Report Year 2023.

The feed itself

A zip archive containing, at minimum:

  • agency.txt, stops.txt, routes.txt, trips.txt, stop_times.txt, and calendar.txt or calendar_dates.txt
  • shapes.txt — now required. FTA finalized this in July 2025, phased by reporter type: it took effect in RY 2025 for Full Reporters and RY 2026 for Reduced, Rural, and Tribal Reporters. The 2026 Policy Manual states it flatly, as required for all GTFS submissions beginning in RY 2026. If you are a Full Reporter, the obligation already applied last cycle. If your feed has never carried route geometry, this is the change most likely to catch you out.
  • feed_info.txt — optional, but FTA strongly recommends it.

Beyond the file list, two content rules do most of the work:

  • No expired service dates. Feeds “should reflect active, current operations,” and calendar.txt or calendar_dates.txt must represent service current at least through the end of your most recently completed fiscal year. FTA points agencies at the MobilityData GTFS Validator and the “Service Window” value at the top of its report as the way to check.
  • agency_id everywhere. Every reporter must supply a value for agency_id on the P-50 form and in agency.txt, routes.txt, and fare_attributes.txt. GTFS treats the field as conditionally required; FTA does not, because it uses the value to crosswalk reporters to routes inside shared feeds. This became a non-conditional NTD requirement in RY 2025.

The link

The feed has to be reachable by an automated process, which rules out several things agencies routinely submit:

  • No enterprise storage links — Google Drive, SharePoint, and similar, because permissions block automated retrieval
  • No links wrapped by email security (Outlook Safe Links and equivalents); submit the direct source URL
  • No password protection; the feed must be publicly accessible
  • The URL must download the zip directly

If hosting a link is genuinely not possible, an agency can request a waiver to submit the zip through the P-50 form instead. National RTAP offers free GTFS hosting to any federally funded agency that cannot host the file itself. You then certify annually on the D-10 form that the feed is accurate and up to date.

How FTA checks it

FTA’s stated primary check is that the links are viable and current: URLs are re-checked periodically, and agencies may be notified when one breaks. Beyond that, validation analysts “manually inspect files and routes, particularly the shapes.txt file,” and FTA has said there will be “some auditing of individual .txt files, though not necessarily for every submission.”

So there is no automated pass/fail validator gate at submission — but there is a human who may open your feed, and a CEO signature on the D-10 attesting that it is accurate and current. FTA encourages agencies to run a GTFS validator before submitting and does not require it; running one anyway is the cheapest way to avoid a validation question you have to answer in writing later.

Who this applies to — and who it doesn’t

NTD reporting itself is broad: if a transit provider, local government, State, or MPO receives or benefits from § 5307 or § 5311 funding, it must report. Since RY 2018 that extends to agencies that receive Chapter 53 funds and own, operate, or manage transit capital assets even without § 5307 or § 5311 money.

The GTFS requirement is narrower. It applies to Full Reporters, Reduced Reporters (including Tribal Reporters), and Rural subrecipient reporters. It does not apply to Reduced Asset Reporters or Group Plan Sponsor Reporters. And within those groups it reaches only reporters that operate a fixed route mode.

Demand response is a different form. Agencies reporting Demand Response modes provide geospatial data on Form B-15 — service area by state, county, and census-designated place, together with days operated, populations served, and fares. That is not a GTFS submission, and NTD does not require GTFS-Flex. An agency that operates only demand-response service has no P-50 GTFS obligation. (Publishing GTFS-Flex can still be worth doing — it is what makes dial-a-ride and microtransit visible in trip planners — but it is a choice, not a federal requirement.)

What a nonconforming submission actually means

This is the part most often overstated, so here is the manual’s own language.

FTA may issue a Failure to Report if an agency fails to submit a report, submits a late report, submits an incomplete report, or fails to respond to validation questions. A report is incomplete if it does not contain all required information, if “the agency did not collect and submit the data in conformance with NTD requirements,” if the required CEO certification and auditor statements are missing, or if the agency does not properly respond to validation questions. When FTA issues the notice, it goes to the agency’s CEO and to the FTA Regional Administrator.

The consequence, stated exactly: “If a transit agency receives a Failure to Report notice, FTA does not include its data in the apportionment of UZA and rural funding.”

That is a data-exclusion consequence, not a funding-eligibility one, and the distinction matters. Elsewhere the same manual is explicit that an approved waiver “does not affect a transit agency’s funding eligibility for § 5307, § 5311, § 5337, or § 5339 funding, but it may affect the amount of funding the agency’s UZA(s) receive. In a large UZA or a rural area, the amount of funding may decrease because FTA may not include specific data in formula funding programs.”

So the honest description of the risk is this: NTD data drives the § 5307 urbanized area formula (and, for urbanized areas under 200,000, the Small Transit Intensive Cities measures), plus § 5337, § 5339, and the § 5311 rural formula. If your service data is excluded, the apportionment to your urbanized area or your state is computed without it. FTA also applies a two-year lag — FY 2026 data are used for FY 2028 apportionment — so the effect is neither immediate nor obvious when it lands.

Nobody sends you an invoice for a broken feed. What happens is quieter and slower: the numbers your region is apportioned on stop reflecting the service you run.

Common questions

Does NTD require a GTFS feed?
Yes, if you operate fixed route service. The 2026 NTD Policy Manual states that FTA requires NTD reporters with fixed route service to create and maintain a GTFS feed, submitted on Form P-50. FTA adopted GTFS reporting as a mandatory requirement in a March 2023 Federal Register notice, beginning in Report Year 2023.

When is the NTD Annual Report due?
Four months after your fiscal year ends. A 30 June fiscal year end means 31 October; 30 September means 31 January; 31 December means 30 April. In FTA’s 2024 Agency Information file, 1,742 of the 2,908 reporter records with a due date — about three in five — were due 31 October.

Is shapes.txt required for NTD reporting?
Yes. FTA finalized the requirement in July 2025 and phased it by reporter type: it took effect in Report Year 2025 for Full Reporters and Report Year 2026 for Reduced, Rural, and Tribal Reporters. The 2026 NTD Policy Manual lists shapes.txt as required for all GTFS submissions beginning in RY 2026.

Does NTD require GTFS for demand-response service?
No. The GTFS requirement on Form P-50 applies to fixed route service. Agencies reporting Demand Response modes provide geospatial data on Form B-15 instead — service area by state, county, and census-designated place, plus days operated, populations served, and fares. GTFS-Flex is not an NTD requirement.

What happens if my NTD submission does not conform to the requirements?
FTA may issue a Failure to Report notice, which it sends to the agency CEO and the FTA Regional Administrator. Per the 2026 NTD Policy Manual, if an agency receives a Failure to Report notice, FTA does not include its data in the apportionment of urbanized area and rural funding. That is exclusion of the agency’s data from the formula factors, not a loss of funding eligibility — FTA states elsewhere in the same manual that a waiver does not affect eligibility for § 5307, § 5311, § 5337, or § 5339 funding, though it may affect the amount the agency’s urbanized area receives.

Can my GTFS feed have expired service dates when I file?
No. The 2026 NTD Policy Manual states that GTFS feeds should reflect active, current operations and should not feature expired service dates, and that agencies must ensure calendar.txt or calendar_dates.txt represent service current at least through the end of the most recently completed fiscal year. FTA points agencies at the MobilityData GTFS Validator and its Service Window value to check this.

Can I upload a GTFS zip instead of hosting a URL?
Only by waiver. FTA expects a publicly accessible link that downloads the zip directly. If an agency demonstrates that hosting a web link is not possible, it can request a waiver for FTA to accept a zip file submission via the P-50 form. National RTAP offers free GTFS hosting to any federally funded agency that cannot host the link itself.

Does FTA validate my GTFS feed?
There is no automated pass/fail validator gate at submission. FTA’s stated primary check is that submitted links are viable and current; URLs are re-checked periodically and agencies may be notified when a link breaks. Validation analysts also manually inspect files and routes, particularly shapes.txt, with some auditing of individual .txt files though not necessarily for every submission. FTA encourages but does not require agencies to run a GTFS validator before submitting, and it requires CEO certification on the D-10 form.
