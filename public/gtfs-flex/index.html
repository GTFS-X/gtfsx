What a Flex feed says

Three things a fixed-route feed cannot express

Ordinary GTFS assumes a vehicle leaves stop A at 8:00 and reaches stop B at 8:07. Your service does not work like that. These are the three shapes Flex adds — and they are in the specification proper, adopted in March 2024 on 18 votes in favour and none against, the most any GTFS change has received. Flex has not been an “extension” since.

A service area, not a stop

Draw the polygon riders can be picked up and dropped off inside — or name a set of existing stops the service will serve. No invented placeholder stops, no fake timepoints.

  • locations.geojson
  • location_groups.txt

A window, and how far ahead to call

Rides run between two times rather than at one time. Alongside it, the rule that actually governs the trip: real-time, same-day with a minimum lead, or prior-day with a cutoff — plus the number riders call and the sentence they should read.

  • start_pickup_drop_off_window
  • end_pickup_drop_off_window
  • booking_rules.txt

A route that bends on request

Deviated fixed-route is a hybrid: the published line and its stops, plus the corridor the bus will leave the line to serve. GTFS·X can generate that corridor by buffering the routes you have already drawn.

  • locations.geojson
  • stop_times.location_id
  • continuous_pickup
Where it shows up

Who reads a Flex feed — and who doesn’t

This is the part worth being exact about, because it is the whole reason to publish. Here is the honest state of it in 2026.

Reads it

Transit

Transit’s partner documentation lists GTFS-Flex support for paratransit, dial-a-ride and deviated fixed-route service, zone-to-zone and point-to-zone, used for service information and trip planning. Be clear-eyed about the limits, which Transit states itself: no real-time pickup ETA, and no booking inside the app — it hands the rider a direct link to your app, website or phone number. Transit’s demand-response documentation

Reads it

OpenTripPlanner 2

The open-source routing engine has supported Flex since OTP 2.1 (March 2022), and its current documentation targets the version merged into the mainline spec in March 2024. One caveat to pass on to whoever runs your regional planner: it is a sandbox feature, so FlexRouting has to be switched on. OTP Flex documentation

Not today

Google Maps

Google’s transit partner documentation says it plainly: “Google Transit does not currently implement the GTFS-Flex specification. GTFS-Flex datasets cannot be accepted at this time.” If anyone has told you a Flex feed will put your dial-a-ride service into Google Maps, they were wrong, and you should hear it here rather than after you publish. Google Transit participation requirements

Also on MobilityData’s Flex adoption tracker: IBI (Arcadis) as a consumer. Separately, transitland-lib added Flex data handling in late 2025 — that is catalogue and data support, not trip planning. Apple Maps and Moovit have published nothing either way, so this page claims nothing about them.

The other half of the answer is durability. A Flex feed is the machine-readable record of a service that currently exists as a phone number and a PDF: a defined service area, the days and hours it runs, and the rule for booking it — in a format the canonical MobilityData validator has checked in full since v7.0 (March 2025), and a catalogue entry any consumer can find. You publish it once; every reader that exists now, and every one that adds support later, gets it.

In the editor

One polygon, one window, one booking rule

That is a complete microtransit feed. Everything below happens on a map in your browser — there is no schema to learn, no CSV to hand-edit, and nothing to install.

Put the service area on the map

Three ways in, and you pick whichever matches how you already think about the service: click out a polygon, group a set of existing stops, or buffer your existing bus routes by a distance you choose — 0.75 miles by default — for deviated-route service.

Creating a zone also creates its paired route_type 715 route. You never add that yourself.

Fill in the window and the booking rule

Pickup start, pickup end, and the calendar the zone runs on. Then the booking rule as a form, not a spec table: real-time, same-day with a minimum lead in minutes, or prior-day with a day-and-time cutoff. Phone and info URL are seeded from your agency.txt record the first time you touch the section.

The window exports as start_pickup_drop_off_window / end_pickup_drop_off_window; the rule becomes a row in booking_rules.txt, referenced from the trip.

Fix what the validator flags, then export

Twenty-six Flex-specific checks run continuously as you edit — zone geometry, ids colliding with stops.txt, a window that ends before it starts, a missing service pattern, and every conditional field that a given booking_type requires or forbids. You are not finding these out after you publish.

Export writes a spec-clean .zip you can host anywhere, mixing flex zones and ordinary fixed routes in one feed.

Common questions

Before you start

Is GTFS-Flex still an extension?
No. It was adopted into the GTFS specification on 18 March 2024 with 18 votes in favour and none against — the most support any GTFS change has received. MobilityData put it plainly at the time: “Now that it’s officially adopted into the spec, Flex is no longer considered an extension.” locations.geojson, location_groups.txt, location_group_stops.txt and booking_rules.txt are all in the reference today, each marked Optional. The spec is still moving, too: safe_duration_factor and safe_duration_offset were added to trips.txt in April 2026, and GTFS·X writes both.
Will publishing Flex put my service in Google Maps?
No. Google’s transit partner documentation states that Google Transit “does not currently implement the GTFS-Flex specification” and that “GTFS-Flex datasets cannot be accepted at this time.” Publishing Flex reaches Transit and OpenTripPlanner-based planners today. We would rather tell you that up front than have you find out afterwards.
Does a Flex feed let riders book a ride?
No, and it is important not to promise your riders otherwise. A Flex feed carries booking instructions — how far ahead to call, by when, and on what number or URL. Apps show that and hand the rider off to your existing booking channel. There is no real-time vehicle availability and no reservation. Real-time on-demand data is a separate standard (GOFS), not this one.
Do I need to be a programmer?
No. Everything is a map and a form: click out a polygon (or buffer routes you already have), type a pickup start and end time, pick a calendar, choose a booking type from three buttons, and export. GTFS·X writes the GeoJSON, the booking rules and the stop_times windows for you. If you would rather not do it at all, we will build the feed for you as a fixed-fee project — talk to us.
Can one feed hold both my fixed routes and my flex service?
Yes, and that is the normal case. Flex zones live in the same project as your ordinary routes, stops and timetables, and export together in one zip. If you already publish a fixed-route feed, adding flex means adding to it, not maintaining a second feed.
